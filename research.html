Research Projects

My research has developed from computational studies of cosmological structure and analogue gravity toward my current work on the cosmological bootstrap. The common thread is a search for controlled ways to connect fundamental principles in quantum fields and gravity with observable signatures.

Current research07/2026 — Present

Cosmological Bootstrap

I study cosmological correlators as probes of inflationary physics. The bootstrap approach uses de Sitter symmetry, locality, unitarity, and analytic structure to constrain observables in the early universe—often without relying on a conventional bulk calculation.

Advisor: Yutin Huang

Past research07/2023 — 07/2026

Particle Creation from Analogue Black Holes

During my master's research, I developed the Inertial Replacement Method (IRM), a hybrid analytic–numerical framework for calculating Bogoliubov coefficients for general moving-mirror trajectories.

  • Derived controlled error estimates for perfectly and imperfectly reflecting mirrors.
  • Validated the method against analytically solvable trajectories.
  • Applied it to a numerical, PIC-based Chen–Mourou plasma-mirror trajectory relevant to AnaBHEL.
  • Found that the radiation spectrum is governed almost entirely by the finite accelerating region.

Advisors: Pisin Chen and Robert M. Wald
